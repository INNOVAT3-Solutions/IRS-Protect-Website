CP3219A: notice of deficiency and the 90-day deadline

CP3219A is an IRS notice of deficiency. It tells you about a proposed increase in your tax, based on information from others such as your employer or bank, and your right to challenge it in the U.S. Tax Court within 90 days. That deadline can't be extended.

Updated: September 29, 2026 · Based on information from IRS.gov · General information, not tax advice.

What it isA notice of deficiency (the “90-day letter”). The IRS says it isn't a bill or an audit.
Deadline90 days to file a Tax Court petition (150 days if the notice is addressed to someone outside the United States). It can't be extended.
If you don't respondIf you don't reply and don't file a petition, the IRS assesses the changes and sends you a bill.
If you agreeSign and return the Form 5564 enclosed with the notice.

The Tax Court deadline can't be extended. According to the IRS, working with it won't extend the time you have to file a petition, and the Tax Court can't consider your case if you file late.

What does a CP3219A mean?

The IRS sends a CP3219A when it decides your tax needs to be adjusted because of information it received from others, such as your employer or bank. It often follows a CP2000 that wasn't answered or resolved. The notice explains the proposed change and how you can challenge it in the U.S. Tax Court.

Your options

  1. If you agree: sign and return Form 5564, Notice of Deficiency – Waiver, enclosed with the notice.
  2. If you disagree: reply right away with a signed statement and supporting documents. The IRS says it can generally accept information over the phone at the number on the notice. You can send documents through the IRS Documents Upload Tool, by fax or by mail, as the notice explains.
  3. If the disagreement isn't resolved: file a petition with the U.S. Tax Court (ustaxcourt.gov) before the last date shown on the notice. The IRS works with you during the 90 days, but that doesn't stop the clock.

About the Tax Court

  • There's a filing fee, which may be waived if you qualify.
  • A simplified small-case procedure applies when the dispute is $50,000 or less for any tax year. A decision under that procedure can't be appealed.
  • The Tax Court's website (ustaxcourt.gov) has guidance for taxpayers representing themselves. You can also be represented by an authorized professional.
