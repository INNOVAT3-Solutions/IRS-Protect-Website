IRS collection notices: CP14, CP501, CP503 and CP504

CP14, CP501, CP503 and CP504 are the letters the IRS sends when you have an unpaid federal tax balance. CP14 is the first bill, CP501 and CP503 are reminders, and CP504 is a notice of intent to levy. Each one has a date on it, so don't set it aside.

Updated: September 29, 2026 · Based on information from IRS.gov · General information, not tax advice.

What they areIRS bills and reminders for unpaid federal taxes, in this order: CP14, CP501, CP503 and CP504.
DeadlineThe due date printed on each notice. CP504 asks you to pay immediately.
If you don't respondPenalties and interest keep adding up, the IRS can file a Notice of Federal Tax Lien, and with CP504 it can levy your state tax refund.
First stepConfirm the balance in your IRS online account, then decide whether to pay, set up a payment plan or get professional help before the date on the notice.

What does each notice mean?

The IRS describes the collection process this way: it sends you a bill; if you don't pay it, it sends at least one more bill while interest and penalties continue to accrue; and if you still don't pay after the final bill, it begins collection actions. The IRS does not publish a fixed number of weeks between notices.

NoticeWhat it isWhat it asks
CP14The first bill: you owe money on unpaid taxes.Pay by the due date on the notice. Interest accrues after that date.
CP501A reminder: the IRS hasn't received your payment or a response to the previous notice.Pay or contact the IRS by the date on the notice.
CP503The second reminder: you still owe a balance.Pay the entire balance by the due date to avoid additional penalties and interest.
CP504Notice of Intent to Levy.Pay the amount shown immediately.

If your address is in a federally declared disaster area, you may receive a CP14C instead of a CP14.

What happens if I don't pay?

  • Penalties and interest. If you can't pay in full by the CP14 due date, a late payment penalty is added, and interest runs until the balance is paid.
  • Tax lien. The IRS can file a Notice of Federal Tax Lien, which can affect your ability to get credit.
  • State refund levy. With CP504, if you don't pay immediately, the IRS can seize any state tax refund you're entitled to. The notice also explains that the IRS can levy other property and income, such as wages, bank accounts, personal assets and Social Security benefits.
  • Final notice and hearing rights. CP504 is not yet the notice that gives you a collection hearing. If the balance stays unpaid, the IRS may send a final notice such as LT11 or Letter 1058, which does, for a short window.
  • Passport. If the debt becomes a “seriously delinquent tax debt” (more than $66,000 in 2026, with a lien filed or a levy issued), the State Department generally won't issue or renew your passport and may revoke it.

What to do, step by step

  1. Confirm the balance is real and correct. Sign in to your IRS online account at IRS.gov and compare the balance and tax year with the notice. Don't use links or phone numbers sent to you by text message or email.
  2. Write down the due date. It's printed on the notice. Interest and penalties keep running while the balance is open.
  3. If you disagree with the amount, contact the IRS at the number on the notice before that date, with your return and the documents that support your position.
  4. If you can pay, pay. The IRS accepts payments at IRS.gov/payments, through your online account, or by mail with the notice's payment stub.
  5. If you can't pay in full, request a payment plan with the Online Payment Agreement application or Form 9465. The IRS also lists the Offer in Compromise and a temporary delay of collection if paying would cause financial hardship.
  6. Get help if you need it. An authorized professional (enrolled agent, CPA or attorney) can deal with the IRS for you. Free help also exists: the Taxpayer Advocate Service (TAS) and Low Income Taxpayer Clinics (LITC).
