LT11 or Letter 1058: final notice of intent to levy

LT11 and Letter 1058 are the final notice the IRS sends before levying property for unpaid taxes. They also tell you about your right to a Collection Due Process hearing, which you generally must request within 30 days of the letter date using Form 12153.

Updated: September 29, 2026 · Based on information from IRS.gov · General information, not tax advice.

What it isThe final notice of intent to levy and of your right to a hearing. The IRS says: “We intend to seize your property or rights to property.”
DeadlineThe date on the letter, generally 30 days from the letter date, to request a Collection Due Process hearing.
If you don't respondThe IRS can levy your wages or bank accounts and file a Notice of Federal Tax Lien.
FormForm 12153, Request for a Collection Due Process or Equivalent Hearing.

This deadline is short and protects important rights. While a Collection Due Process hearing about a levy is pending, the IRS is generally prohibited from levying your property. Miss the deadline and you lose the right to take the case to court.

What does LT11 or Letter 1058 mean?

It arrives after bills and reminders went unpaid (for example, notices CP14 through CP504). The IRS explains that it hasn't received your payment for overdue taxes and intends to seize your property or rights to property. According to Publication 594, the IRS normally sends this final notice at least 30 days before a levy.

CP90 and CP297 are similar final notices: the IRS also lists them as a final notice of intent to levy with hearing rights, the same 30-day window and the same Form 12153.

What is a Collection Due Process hearing?

It's an independent review of your case by the IRS Independent Office of Appeals before the levy. You can, for example, propose another way to pay or explain why you disagree with the collection action. You request it with Form 12153 within the window on the letter.

  • On time (generally 30 days): a Collection Due Process hearing, with the right to take the case to the U.S. Tax Court if you disagree with the decision.
  • Late, but within one year of the notice date: you can request an Equivalent Hearing, but you can't go to court.

What to do, step by step

  1. Find the deadline on the letter. Count from the letter date, not from the day you opened it.
  2. Confirm the balance. Sign in to your IRS online account at IRS.gov. If the letter looks suspicious, call the IRS at 800-829-1040.
  3. Decide whether to request a hearing. If you disagree with the levy or need another payment option, send Form 12153 before the deadline, following the letter's instructions.
  4. If you can pay, pay. Once the balance is paid in full, the IRS stops adding interest and applicable penalties.
  5. If you can't pay in full, the IRS points to payment plans (online or with Form 9465). Publication 594 also describes the Offer in Compromise and “currently not collectible” status.
  6. Get professional help early. An enrolled agent, CPA or attorney can request the hearing and represent you. Free help is also available from the Taxpayer Advocate Service (TAS) and Low Income Taxpayer Clinics (LITC).
