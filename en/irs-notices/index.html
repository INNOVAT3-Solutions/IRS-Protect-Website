IRS notice guides

Got a letter from the IRS? What to do

If you got a letter from the IRS, don't ignore it and don't panic. Find the notice number (for example, CP2000 or LTR 566) in the upper or lower right corner, confirm the letter is real on IRS.gov, and respond by the date it shows. Each notice has its own deadlines and options.

Updated: September 29, 2026 · Based on information from IRS.gov · General information, not tax advice.

What to do when an IRS letter arrives

  1. Find the notice number. It's in the corner of the letter, such as “CP14” or “LTR 1058.” That number tells you what the IRS is asking for.
  2. Confirm it's real. Look up the notice on IRS.gov or sign in to your IRS online account, where you can view digital notices. If it isn't there or looks suspicious, call the IRS at 800-829-1040. The IRS starts contact by mail, and a legitimate IRS link includes “https” and “irs.gov.”
  3. Write down the deadline. Almost every notice has a date to reply or pay. Responding on time protects your appeal rights.
  4. Read what it asks and compare it with your return. Keep the return for that year and your W-2 and 1099 forms at hand.
  5. Respond or get help before the date. An enrolled agent, CPA or attorney can represent you before the IRS. Free help is also available from the Taxpayer Advocate Service and Low Income Taxpayer Clinics.
