What it isA proposal to change your return (CP2000 series, CP2000A through CP2000E). The IRS says it isn't a bill.
Why you got itForms sent by your employer, bank or other payers (W-2, 1099 and similar) don't match your return.
DeadlineThe reply date listed on the notice. You can ask for more time.
If you don't respondThe IRS may send another notice and a bill for the proposed change.

What does a CP2000 mean?

The IRS compares your return with information third parties send it. When something doesn't match (for example, a 1099 from freelance work you didn't include), it sends a CP2000 with the change it proposes. The difference may increase your tax, decrease it, or not change it at all. The notice includes a response form where you say whether you agree.

Sometimes the IRS first sends a simpler CP2501 asking you to explain the difference. If you agree with that notice, the IRS computes the additional tax and sends you a CP2000.

How to respond, step by step

  1. Read each proposed change. Compare every item on the notice with your return and your W-2 and 1099 forms for that year.
  2. Decide whether you agree. If you agree with everything, sign the response form and send it with payment or your payment plan request. You don't need to amend your return.
  3. If you disagree with all or part of it, check the disagree box, explain each item in a signed statement and attach copies of supporting documents. If you filed jointly, both spouses must sign.
  4. If you have other income, credits or expenses to report, file Form 1040-X and write “CP2000” at the top.
  5. Send your response by the date on the notice. The IRS says the fastest way is the IRS Documents Upload Tool with the access code on your notice. You can also fax it to the number for the IRS center shown on the notice, or mail it to the address on page 1.
  6. If you need more time, ask before the deadline: by mail or fax with a reply option, or by calling the toll-free number on the notice.

Watch out for fake links. The IRS says a legitimate upload link includes “https” and “irs.gov.” If the notice looks suspicious, look it up on IRS.gov or call the IRS at 800-829-1040.

What happens if I don't respond?

If you don't reply or the IRS can't resolve the discrepancy, it may send another notice and a bill. That next notice can be a notice of deficiency such as CP3219A, which gives you 90 days to go to the U.S. Tax Court before the IRS assesses the tax.
